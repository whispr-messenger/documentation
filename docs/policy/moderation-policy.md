# Whispr Community Moderation Policy

## Purpose

This document defines the rules, categories, and procedures that govern
content moderation on the Whispr messaging platform. It applies to all
users, moderators, and administrators. The goal is to maintain a safe
and respectful communication environment while being transparent about
enforcement practices.

---

## 1. Report Categories

Users can report messages or users for the following reasons. Each
category has specific criteria that moderators use when reviewing
reports.

### 1.1 Offensive Content

Content that is deliberately hurtful, derogatory, or discriminatory.

**Includes:**
- Slurs, epithets, or dehumanizing language targeting individuals or
  groups based on race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender,
  sexual orientation, disability, or other protected characteristics.
- Deliberate misgendering or deadnaming.
- Content promoting supremacist ideologies.

**Does not include:**
- Strong language that is not directed at a person or group.
- Disagreements or heated debates that remain respectful.
- Quoting offensive content for the purpose of discussing or
  condemning it (context matters).

### 1.2 Spam

Unsolicited, repetitive, or commercial content that disrupts
conversations.

**Includes:**
- Repeated identical or near-identical messages across conversations.
- Unsolicited advertising, promotional links, or commercial solicitation.
- Bot-generated content or automated message flooding.
- Phishing links or deceptive URLs.
- Pyramid schemes, scam promotions, or fraudulent offers.

**Does not include:**
- Sharing a link once in a relevant conversation.
- Enthusiastic messages that happen to be repetitive (e.g., celebrating
  a sports event).

### 1.3 Nudity / Sexual Content

Sexually explicit material or non-consensual intimate content.

**Includes:**
- Sexually explicit images, videos, or text descriptions.
- Non-consensual intimate imagery (revenge porn).
- Sexual content involving minors (zero tolerance -- immediate ban
  and report to authorities).
- Unsolicited sexual messages or sexual advances after being asked
  to stop.

**Does not include:**
- Non-sexual nudity in an artistic or educational context (e.g.,
  medical discussion).
- Consensual flirtatious conversation between adults in private
  messages (unless one party reports it).

### 1.4 Violence

Content that promotes, glorifies, or threatens physical violence.

**Includes:**
- Direct threats of physical harm against a specific person or group.
- Graphic depictions of violence intended to shock or intimidate.
- Instructions for carrying out violent acts.
- Content glorifying terrorism or mass violence.
- Encouraging self-harm or suicide.

**Does not include:**
- News reporting or discussion of violent events.
- Historical or educational content about violence.
- Fictional violence in the context of storytelling or media
  discussion.

### 1.5 Harassment

Targeted, persistent behavior intended to intimidate, control, or
distress another user.

**Includes:**
- Repeated unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Doxxing (sharing personal information without consent).
- Stalking behavior (tracking a user across conversations).
- Coordinated harassment campaigns against a user.
- Intimidation or blackmail.
- Impersonation of another user.

**Does not include:**
- A single unwelcome message (unless it contains threats or other
  category violations).
- Disagreements or criticism that do not constitute a pattern.

### 1.6 Other

Reports that do not fit the above categories. The reporter must
provide a description explaining the concern. Moderators evaluate
these on a case-by-case basis.

## 2. Sanction Severity Guidelines

Sanctions are applied based on the severity and frequency of
violations. The following guidelines help moderators maintain
consistency.

### 2.1 Warning

**When to use:**
- First offense for minor violations.
- Borderline cases where intent is unclear.
- Content that technically violates policy but causes minimal harm.

**Examples:**
- First instance of mildly offensive language.
- Sharing a promotional link without context.
- A single unwanted message that stops after the warning.

### 2.2 Temporary Mute

**When to use:**
- Repeated minor violations after a warning.
- A single moderate violation.
- Auto-escalation trigger (3 reports in 7 days).

**Duration guidelines:**
- Minor repeated offense: 24 hours.
- Moderate single offense: 48-72 hours.
- Severe but non-ban-worthy offense: 7 days.

**Examples:**
- Continued spam after a warning.
- Sending offensive content to multiple conversations.
- Persistent unwanted contact (but not threatening).

### 2.3 Ban

**When to use:**
- Severe violations regardless of prior history.
- Repeated moderate violations despite previous sanctions.
- Auto-escalation trigger (5 reports in 14 days).

**Immediate ban triggers (no warning required):**
- Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) -- also reported to authorities.
- Direct, credible threats of violence.
- Doxxing (sharing personal information).
- Non-consensual intimate imagery.

**Examples:**
- Sharing CSAM (immediate permanent ban + law enforcement report).
- Sending death threats.
- Repeated harassment campaign after mute periods.

### 2.4 Sanction Escalation Ladder

For users who repeatedly violate community guidelines:

```
First offense       --> Warning
Second offense      --> 24-hour mute
Third offense       --> 7-day mute
Fourth offense      --> 30-day mute
Fifth offense       --> Permanent ban
```

Moderators may skip steps based on severity. The auto-escalation
system operates independently of this ladder based on report
thresholds.

## 3. Appeal Process

### 3.1 Who Can Appeal

Any user who has received an active sanction (warning, mute, or ban)
can submit one appeal per sanction.

### 3.2 How to Appeal

1. Navigate to the moderation section in user settings.
2. Select the active sanction to appeal.
3. Provide a written explanation of why the sanction should be
   reversed.
4. Optionally attach supporting evidence (screenshots, context).
5. Submit the appeal.

### 3.3 Appeal Review Process

1. Appeals are assigned to an admin who was not involved in the
   original sanction decision.
2. The reviewer examines:
   - The original report and evidence snapshot.
   - The sanction details and reason.
   - The appeal reasoning and any new evidence.
   - The user's moderation history and reputation.
3. The reviewer decides to accept or reject the appeal with a
   written explanation.
4. If accepted: the sanction is immediately lifted and the user's
   reputation score is adjusted upward.
5. If rejected: the sanction remains in effect and the decision is
   final for that sanction.

### 3.4 Appeal Timelines

- Appeals should be reviewed within 24 hours for mute sanctions
  (since the mute may expire before review).
- Appeals for bans should be reviewed within 72 hours.
- Auto-sanction appeals should be prioritized as they are more
  likely to be false positives.

### 3.5 Appeal Limits

- One appeal per sanction.
- Maximum 3 appeals per day per user (across all sanctions).
- No re-appeal after rejection.

## 4. Admin and Moderator Responsibilities

### 4.1 Moderator Responsibilities

Moderators are trusted community members with the following duties:

1. **Review reports promptly**: Aim to review pending reports within
   4 hours during active moderation shifts.
2. **Apply sanctions consistently**: Follow the severity guidelines
   in Section 2. Document the reasoning in admin notes.
3. **Preserve evidence**: Never modify or delete evidence. If
   additional context is needed, add admin notes.
4. **Escalate when uncertain**: If a report involves legal issues
   (threats, CSAM, doxxing), escalate to an admin immediately.
5. **Maintain neutrality**: Do not moderate reports involving
   personal friends or enemies. Recuse yourself and let another
   moderator handle it.
6. **Respect confidentiality**: Do not share report details, user
   identities, or moderation decisions outside the moderation team.

### 4.2 Admin Responsibilities

Admins have all moderator responsibilities plus:

1. **Review appeals**: Process appeals within the timelines defined
   in Section 3.4.
2. **Manage moderator team**: Assign and revoke moderator roles.
   Monitor moderator activity for consistency.
3. **Adjust thresholds**: Review auto-escalation thresholds monthly
   and adjust based on false positive/negative rates.
4. **Audit trail review**: Periodically review the audit log for
   anomalies (e.g., a moderator applying an unusual number of
   sanctions).
5. **Legal compliance**: Ensure moderation practices comply with
   applicable laws (EU Digital Services Act, local regulations).
6. **Report to authorities**: When content involves imminent danger,
   CSAM, or other legally mandated reporting situations, follow the
   incident response procedure.

### 4.3 Prohibited Moderator Actions

- Sanctioning users for personal reasons.
- Sharing moderation data with unauthorized parties.
- Modifying or deleting reports or evidence.
- Applying sanctions without documenting the reason.
- Ignoring reports from specific users or about specific categories.

## 5. Transparency

### 5.1 User Visibility

Users can see:
- Their own active sanctions and the reasons.
- Whether a sanction was manual or automatic.
- The status of their appeals.
- Their reputation score (future feature).

Users cannot see:
- Who reported them.
- Internal admin notes.
- Other users' sanctions or reports.

### 5.2 Platform Transparency

Whispr publishes (or will publish) periodic transparency reports
including:
- Total number of reports received and their categories.
- Number of sanctions applied (manual vs. automatic).
- Number of appeals and their outcomes.
- Average response times for report review and appeal decisions.

---

## 6. Policy Updates

This policy may be updated as the platform evolves. Changes will be:
- Announced to users via in-app notification.
- Documented with an effective date.
- Applied prospectively (not retroactively to past moderation
  decisions).
